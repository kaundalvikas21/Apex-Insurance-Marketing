Amounts between and above these columns are written every day. Ten and twenty five thousand dollars are shown because that is the band most policies in this category fall into, not because they are the only options. Below about five thousand dollars few carriers will write at all, and above about fifty thousand you are usually better served by a standard whole life insurance policy, which is the same contract priced for a larger market.

What actually moves the price

In rough order of size. The first two are worth more than everything below them put together, which is why the chart is built around them.

Your age when the policy is issued

Not your age when you decide, your age when the carrier issues the policy. It is the largest single factor, and it is the only one on this list that gets worse while you think about it.

How much coverage you buy

The premium tracks the face amount closely on this product, more closely than it does on a large underwritten policy. Halving the coverage roughly halves the payment.

Tobacco and nicotine use

A meaningful step up for the same coverage, carried for life rather than for a term. Most carriers look at the last twelve months, and they define tobacco more widely than people expect.

Your health answers

They decide which of three things you are offered: a level benefit policy that pays in full from day one, a graded policy that pays partially at first, or a guaranteed acceptance policy with a full waiting period. The gap between the first and the last is large.

Which carrier writes you

Carriers disagree sharply about the same medication and the same diagnosis. Two quotes for one person on one day can land a long way apart, and neither company is being unreasonable.

How you pay

Monthly payment usually carries a modal factor, so twelve monthly payments cost a little more than one annual one. Small next to the items above, and it is real money you can keep by asking.

How to bring a quoted price down

All of these are legitimate, and the first one is worth more than the other four together.

Five things worth trying, in the order we would try them. None of them involves leaving anything off an application.

  • Let us try more than one carrier. The same health history can produce a level benefit policy at one carrier and a graded one at another. This is the single biggest lever on this page and it is the one you cannot pull on your own.
  • Size the policy to the job. Work out what the funeral and the bills would actually come to and insure that, rather than picking a round number. It is usually a bigger saving than any class change.
  • Ask what each rider costs as a line. Accidental death and child riders are added easily and removed just as easily. Anything you cannot explain the purpose of back to the agent is a line you can probably drop.
  • Pay annually if you can. Most carriers charge a modal factor for monthly payment. Paying yearly, or even quarterly, is a real discount rather than a sales tactic.
  • Buy sooner rather than later. Not a sales line, an arithmetic one. The premium is set by your age at issue, so a year of thinking about it costs a year of pricing, permanently.

If a waiting period is the thing pushing your price up, read burial insurance with no waiting period before you accept it. Some carriers will write day one coverage for histories that others grade.

Why your quote may not match this chart

A cost chart is an illustration of shape. It shows how premiums move between ages and coverage amounts. It cannot show what a carrier will decide about you, and any chart that claims otherwise is selling you a number it does not have.

A chart assumes an acceptance

Usually a level benefit policy for someone in reasonable health. A graded or guaranteed acceptance policy is priced above this, and that is a normal outcome rather than a bad one.

Carriers differ by state

Product availability, riders, minimum face amounts, and pricing all vary. The carrier that is cheapest in one state may not write in yours at all.

Your medications are read together

Not one at a time. A combination can move you between offers even when no single item on the list would. A chart cannot know any of it.

Questions about the cost

How much does final expense insurance cost per month?

It depends on four things: your age when the policy is issued, how much coverage you buy, whether you use tobacco, and your answers to the health questions. That is why this page is a chart rather than a single figure. What is fair to say is the shape. The premium rises steadily with age and rises sharply after about seventy five, it roughly tracks the coverage amount, and once it is set it never moves again for as long as the policy is in force.

Does the premium go up as I get older?

No. That is the defining feature of the product and the main reason people buy it at this age. The premium is calculated once, from your age and health at the time the policy is issued, and it is guaranteed not to rise. What rises is the price of buying a new policy later, which is a different thing and is the real cost of waiting.

Why is the price so much higher at 80 than at 60?

Because the carrier is pricing the years between now and a claim, and at eighty there are fewer of them. It is the same coverage doing the same job, collected over a shorter time. This is also why the advice to buy the amount you can carry rather than the largest amount you qualify for gets more important with every year.

How much does tobacco use add?

Commonly a meaningful step up in premium for the same coverage, and you carry the difference for the rest of your life rather than for a term. Most carriers ask about the last twelve months. A few will reconsider the class after a documented period without nicotine, which is worth asking about rather than assuming.

Can I lower the price I have been quoted?

Usually, and there are five legitimate ways to try, all of them on this page. None of them involves leaving something off an application. Answering a health question wrongly is not a discount, it is a reason for the carrier to contest the claim, and the person who pays for that is your family.

Is it worth buying at 80?

Sometimes yes and sometimes no, and we will say which on the call rather than in a brochure. It is worth it when your family would otherwise have to find the money quickly and the premium fits comfortably in your budget. It is not worth it when the premium would strain a fixed income, because a policy that lapses at eighty five has cost you every payment and paid nothing back.
